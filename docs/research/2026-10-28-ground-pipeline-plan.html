Ground Pipeline Plan

Jeff asked whether there is a better way to generate and bake the ground. This page maps how the fork builds it today, why its known problems happen, what big open-world games and sims do instead, what BeamNG 0.40 lets a mod do, and a staged plan.

The short answer

Yes. The biggest gain is not a new renderer. It is changing where and when the ground is computed.

  • Build the ground once, in world space. Today every block, road plan and far tile is built in the frame of whichever anchor the car is near, so the same ground is built again for each anchor, and a bake depends on the path driven. Studios keep the data in a fixed world grid and apply the origin shift only when drawing.
  • Split it into layers that each have their own version. Elevation, the road network, the graded ground and the photo should be separate stages with their own cache keys, like Unreal's landscape edit layers. Then a change to signs or buildings stops rebaking the terrain.
  • Make the road profile the master. Solve each road's height along its whole length, bridges included, with real design limits on grade and on how fast the grade may change. The terrain then conforms to the roads. That removes the short ramps at bridge ends and the humps under overpasses at their source, where today they are patched one rule at a time.

BeamNG limits the rendering side more than the data side: one colliding terrain block, no virtual texturing, and a terrain shader whose detail stops at 250 m. Within those limits the block edge can be made far less visible by giving both sides of it the same photo and material treatment.

1How the ground is built today

Everything is computed by the companion server (Python, plus the earthfast native module) and handed to the game as files. In "detail" mode, the mode start.py uses, the car drives on one TerrainBlock that is also drawn, and mesh tiles draw everything beyond it.

4What BeamNG 0.40 lets a mod do

From the fork's own probes (Oct 9–12), the game's shipped shader sources, and the look thread's custom post effect.

CapabilityDetail
One colliding TerrainBlockyesFlat, axis-aligned, at most 8192². Only the first block collides. Creating a second block while a car exists drops the car through the ground. Collision follows a .ter swap or setHeight edit by the next frame without reloadCollision.
Bigger blockcostly.ter swap: 512² 10–15 ms, 1024² 26–38 ms, 2048² ~92 ms on the main thread. Rendering is cheap (+0.5 ms for 4096² at 1 m).

Our own shadersplausibleThe game ships plain HLSL in shaders/common, and the look thread already ships its own compute shader. Overriding terrain/terrainImpl.hlsl from the mod (to add, say, a far photo layer) looks possible but is untested and would break on game updates.
Virtual texturingnoNo engine support. 2048² is the practical texture size to stream; a 4096² texture costs an ~82 ms frame.
MeshesyesServer-written .cdae TSStatics load in 2–4 ms and can be placed with any rotation, which is what tile-local frames need. Collision rebuilds are global and grow with every collidable triangle, so collidable meshes stay few and near.
Native patcheslast resortPossible (the Linux grass patch did it), but tied to one game build. Windows needs DLL injection. Candidates: an asynchronous terrain import, an incremental collision rebuild.

5Target design: a world ground store

Four stages, each a pure function of its inputs with its own version number, stored per world tile on a fixed grid. Each tile keeps its own small local frame. Elevation is stored as height above the ellipsoid, which does not depend on any frame. The anchor only enters at the last, cheap step.

A · Elevation tiles

world grid, e.g. 1 km tiles at 0.5–1 m

The merged best source per tile (3DEP 1 m lidar, Mapterhorn z16–17, fallbacks), with the sea carve and runway flattening applied. It only changes when a source or its version does, so it is effectively permanent.

B · Road network with a profile solver

per connected corridor, world space

Plan the horizontal geometry as today, then solve height along each whole road chain, ground and bridge parts together, in one least-squares fit:

  • Fit the lidar where the road sits on ground. Fit deck lidar (3DEP class 17) on bridges. Give zero weight to the interpolated DEM under decks.
  • Limit grade by class, and limit how fast the grade may change, which is the "K value" of a highway vertical curve. At 70 mph a crest needs on the order of 70–80 m per 1 % of grade change. That single limit is what rules out a 1 m dive over 10 m.
  • Keep at least 5 m clearance (16 ft, the US interstate minimum) at every crossing as an inequality, so the lower road dips only as much as it must, over a length the K value allows.
  • Give at-grade junctions one shared height. Make bridge ends tangent to their approaches.

Output: 3D centrelines, widths and cross-section templates. Ground tiles, road meshes, decks and the AI graph all read the same splines, as they read the same grading today.

C · Ground tiles

world grid, heights + material ids

A plus each road's corridor: the template across the carriageway, then an embankment that blends to the natural ground, the same idea as an Unreal spline layer. Lots, gas station pads and plazas are flatten layers. Stored as rasters for the drawn ground and far tiles. The physics block applies the same corridors point by point from the shared plans instead, because the prototype showed rasters smear graded walls (section 8).

D · Photo pyramid

world quadtree, 0.5 m at the bottom

De-shadowed, seam-matched imagery with roofs filled and road surfaces painted, BC7 encoded with mips. The block's base photo and every far tile's texture become crops of the same pyramid, which removes the separate rendering paths behind the block-edge mismatch and takes far textures out of the server process.

Per-anchor assembly

milliseconds, not seconds

The block resamples C at its 2 m lattice in the anchor frame (the existing elev(lat, lon) interface, fed from tiles instead of from grading). Far tiles and road tiles are built in their own tile frames and placed with a rotation and translation. Moving between ENU frames is a rigid transform, so this is exact, and a rebase keeps every tile already loaded instead of re-streaming ~300.

8Prototype: world ground tiles

Question: can the physics block be a cheap resample of world-space graded tiles instead of being graded per anchor, without losing accuracy? The test grades a stretch of I-25 once in a world tile, then builds the same block for two different anchors from it, and compares against today's direct build for each anchor.

Setup: I-25 at 6th Avenue, a 512² block at 2 m, built for the route's pinned anchors 3 and 2 (2 km and 13 km away). DEM z16 and map data from the PC's cache, offline. Tool: tools/world_ground_proto.py on branch feature/world-ground.

Way of building the blockTimeHeights vs today's direct build
Today: a grader per anchor9.2 s (anchor 3), 7.8 s (anchor 2); each anchor plans the same roads againreference
One grader in a fixed region frame, shared by both anchors8.7 s once, then 0.2 s for the other anchoranchor 3: max 1.7 cm. Anchor 2: p99 0.3 cm; 166 samples differ by 10–52 cm (see below)
Graded ground stored as a raster, resampled7–10 s once (1 m / 0.5 m), then 0.04 s per anchor0.5 m raster: p99 0.9–1.2 cm, but 57–100 samples off by 0.5–4 m

Grading once per place works and is exact. A grader on a fixed region frame gives the same heights as today's to within 2 cm and makes every other anchor's block 40 times cheaper. In a bake, each place's road plans would be made once instead of once per anchor that sees it.

Today's per-anchor grading is not consistent. Graded in anchor 2's frame, two spots near the interchange come out up to 2.4 m different from anchor 3's for the same ground. So after a rebase the ground can change under a car at a few places. Region frames remove that by construction.

Rasters are the wrong store for the physics ground. They are accurate almost everywhere, but the retaining walls and cut slopes that grading makes are near vertical, and bilinear lookups smear them by metres (65–92 % of the bad samples sit on slopes steeper than 1:2). Going to 0.25 m only halves it. So the block should keep applying road corridors from shared plans point by point (0.2 s), and rasters should serve the drawn ground and far tiles, where a smeared wall is invisible.

This changes the first step of stage 1: move the graders from anchor frames to a fixed lattice of region frames and key the shared plan cache by region. That is days of work, not weeks, and it delivers most of stage 1's speed and all of its consistency. It touches grading.py, which the bridges thread is changing tonight, so it should start after that lands.
